# E-mu format front panel (experimental, one-off) — 6" × 6", 1/4" jacks

A vintage E-mu Modular style panel for the Strämpler main board, for the
local hackerspace's E-mu system. Not a board-house part: cut it from
aluminum on the mill or from plastic on the laser, then mark or engrave
the artwork. Generated by `gen_emu_panel.py` (parametric — edit the CONFIG
block, rerun, all files regenerate).

| file | what |
|---|---|

## Format facts (E-mu Modular, 1970s)

- Modules are **6" high**, widths in **3" steps** (3 / 6 / 12"). The board
  footprint is 91.3 mm ≈ 3.6", so this panel is one 6" × 6" unit.
- Natural bright aluminum, black lettering, **blue accent lines**.
- **Mounting holes are a PLACEHOLDER** (4 × Ø4.2 at 0.25" from each
  corner). Measure the hackerspace cabinet's rail hole spacing and screw
  size before cutting, then set `MOUNT_HOLES` / `MOUNT_DIA`.

## Panel thickness — the real constraint

The board's pots and encoder pass **through** this panel and are held by
their own nuts, so the panel can't be thicker than their bushings allow:

| part | bushing | max sensible panel |
|---|---|---|
| Same Sky PTN091/PTN092 pots (POT1–5) | M7 × **5 mm** long | **≤ 2.0 mm** (5 − 2.5 mm nut − thread engagement) |
| EC12 encoder (SW1) | M7, ~7 mm | 3 mm |
| Switchcraft 1/4" jacks | 3/8"-32, 0.276" (7 mm) | 3 mm |
| SMA bulkhead (ANT) | 1/4"-36, ~7 mm | 3 mm |

So: **1.5–2.0 mm aluminum** (1/16" = 1.6 mm is ideal, same as the FR4
panel), or 2 mm acrylic at most. 1/8" (3.2 mm) aluminum or 3 mm acrylic
will leave the pot nuts with no thread. If the shop only has 3 mm stock,
counterbore the six pot/encoder holes from the back to ~1.6 mm.

Board-locked cutouts (display window, 4 CV pots, gain pot, encoder, two
buttons, LED, antenna, five M3 panel screws) are copied verbatim from the
Eurorack panel `hardware-kicad/strampler_panel_v2_3.kicad_pcb` and only
translated by `BOARD_ORIGIN`. Countersink the five M3 holes if the panel
is aluminum (they take the panel-to-standoff screws).

Omitted on purpose: the Eurorack rail holes and the fourteen 3.5 mm jack
holes. The SD card slot (2.5 × 13 mm at the left edge) is **kept** — set
`INCLUDE_SD_SLOT = False` to drop it if the card is to stay inside. (The
TFT module's own SD socket is **not wired** to the ESP32; using it would
mean running the SD lines to it.)

## 1/4" jacks — wiring

The board's PJ398SM pads stay unpopulated; each 1/4" jack gets two flying
leads (tip → `IN` pad, sleeve → `GND` pad). The `NORM` (switch) pad is
tied to ground on the board, so **plain 2-lug TS jacks are all that's
needed** — no switching contact. Twist tip/sleeve pairs; audio in/out and
the two V/oct inputs get the shortest, tidiest runs.

| jack | signal | board pad (panel x, y mm) | jack hole (x, y mm) | lead ≈ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J5 | TRIG 1 | 75.02, 47.35 | 15.40, 118.60 | 108 mm |
| J6 | TRIG 2 | 75.02, 34.65 | 39.40, 118.60 | 106 mm |
| J7 | CV 1 (V/oct) | 87.74, 47.35 | 15.40, 93.20 | 101 mm |
| J8 | CV 2 (V/oct) | 100.40, 47.35 | 39.40, 93.20 | 91 mm |
| J11 | CV 3 (±5 V) | 113.20, 47.35 | 15.40, 67.80 | 115 mm |
| J12 | CV 4 (±5 V) | 125.93, 47.35 | 39.40, 67.80 | 104 mm |
| J2 | IN L | 62.29, 47.35 | 15.40, 42.40 | 62 mm |
| J1 | IN R | 62.29, 34.65 | 39.40, 42.40 | 39 mm |
| J4 | OUT L | 138.65, 47.35 | 15.40, 17.00 | 142 mm |

Layout (Arlo 2026-09-07): **CV 5–8 jacks in a row across the bottom,
each directly below its knob** (same x as POT2–5), in the strip under the
PCB's bottom edge (the PCB is 108 mm tall inside the 128.5 mm panel
region, so the board region is raised to y = 18 and the jack row sits at
y = 17 with ~5 mm nut clearance to the PCB edge). The other ten jacks are
a 2 × 5 field on the left at 1" pitch — TRIG 1/2, CV 1/2, CV 3/4, IN L/R,
OUT L/R — with OUT sharing the bottom row's height. Three rounded blue
boxes: left field, interface block, CV 5–8 row; wordmark "CTAG
STRÄMPLER" alone between.

Jack field geometry: left field two columns 24 mm apart, five rows at 1"
pitch; bottom row on the knob spacing (21.2 mm). Ø9.6 holes (3/8"-32
bushing). Plugs clear each other at both pitches. Switchcraft bodies are 0.5" wide × 0.62"
tall behind the panel — orient the lugs toward the board.

## Jack sourcing (14 needed, buy 16)

Period-correct is the Switchcraft open-frame family; the panel doesn't
need the switch contact, so the 2-lug versions are the right ones.
Prices are 2026 list-ish from the usual dealers — verify at order time.

| option | part | type | ≈ each | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **vintage-correct** | **Switchcraft 11** | open frame, mono, 2 lugs | $3–4 | the classic 70s American-modular jack; Amplified Parts, Mouser 502-11 |
| enclosed, same feel | Switchcraft 111 (111X) | enclosed, mono, 2 lugs | $4 | dust-proof; same bushing |
| switching (not needed) | Switchcraft 12A / 112A | open / enclosed, shunt tip, 3 lugs | $4–5 | only if a future board uses NORM |
| budget | Rean NYS229 / Neutrik NJ2FD | enclosed, mono, solder lugs | $1.5–2 | Mouser/Digi-Key, fine electrically |
| cheapest | generic open-frame TS, AliExpress | | <$1 | check the bushing is 3/8"-32 |

All of these use the **3/8"-32 bushing → Ø9.5 mm hole** (the file uses
9.6 for clearance). Knurled nut + lock washer come with the Switchcrafts.

## Open items before cutting

1. Cabinet mounting-hole pattern (measure → `MOUNT_HOLES`, `MOUNT_DIA`).
2. Panel stock thickness (see table; 1.6 mm aluminum preferred).
3. Whether the shop's laser can mark aluminum (fibre / CerMark) or the
   artwork goes on as a print / engrave / vinyl.
